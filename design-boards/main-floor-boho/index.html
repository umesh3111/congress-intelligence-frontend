RULE 01

Three textures, no more

Per room: pick three materials to repeat (say oak, bouclé, jute). Boho tips into clutter fast — the edit is the design.

RULE 02

Light & leggy

These rooms are 79–165 sq ft. Raised legs, open arms and cane backs let light and floor show through, so pieces read smaller.

RULE 03

One clay moment

Let a single terracotta gesture lead each room — a rug, a wall, a throw. Everything else stays quiet and warm-neutral.

RULE 04

Warm metals, high drapery

Swap chrome for matte black or unlacquered brass. Hang linen curtains near the ceiling and past the trim to lift low rooms.

Concept · Foyer & hall
ROOM 01Foyer + central hall · staircase sightline

A calm, grounded welcome

The first three seconds of the house. Keep the sightline from the front door clear and let one warm vignette do the talking — wood, a woven basket, something green.

Retire
  • Any bulky coat armoire or dark console that narrows the entry
  • Builder-grade flush ceiling light & chrome hooks
Bring in
  • Slim oak + cane console on legs (12–14" deep) with a woven catch-all basket beneath
  • Arched rattan mirror to bounce daylight down the hall
  • Woven pendant or rattan flush-mount + matte-black wall hooks
  • Olive stoneware vase with pampas; low terracotta-striped runner

Small footprint — everything hugs one wall so the door swing and stair stay open.

Concept · Living
ROOM 0212'5" × 10'1" · 121 sq ft

An airy conversation nook

A tight front room, so it earns its keep as an intimate two-seat lounge. Float a compact sofa, add one sculptural cane chair, and keep the centre of the floor visible.

Retire
  • Oversized sofa/sectional and dark, closed-front coffee table
  • Short curtains hung at the window frame
Bring in
  • Oatmeal bouclé loveseat (≤ 72"), raised wood legs
  • Cane & rattan lounge chair for texture without bulk
  • Round travertine or oak drum table — no hard corners in a small room
  • Layered jute + wool flatweave; olive linen drapes hung high & wide
  • Clay throw, one olive cushion, trailing pothos

10-ft wall is the constraint: leggy pieces + a mirror opposite the window keep it breathing.

Concept · Family room
ROOM 0314'3" × 10'4" · 143 sq ft

The textural anchor lounge

The biggest everyday living space, so this is where layering earns its place — a linen sofa, a leather pouf, an olive tree and a low slatted media wall, all kept in the same warm family.

Retire
  • Tall dark entertainment unit and glossy black media furniture
  • Matchy "living-room set" upholstery
Bring in
  • Low oak slat media console — long and grounded, not tall
  • Linen or bouclé sofa + a leather pouf that doubles as a footrest
  • Floating oak shelves styled with stoneware, books, one clay vessel
  • Big jute rug, woven arc floor lamp, potted olive tree
  • Mixed pillows: mudcloth, bouclé, terracotta velvet (max three)

Layer, but audit: three textures repeated reads intentional; five reads busy.

Concept · Kitchen & breakfast nook
ROOM 04Kitchen 79 sq ft · Breakfast nook 105 sq ft

Warm, handmade, light on its feet

A compact kitchen that opens to the breakfast nook — treat them as one bright, tactile zone. Keep the boxes, change the surfaces, and let the nook carry the softness.

Retire
  • Cold hardware, orange-toned wood tones, glossy tile
  • The formal breakfast set; heavy overhead fixture
Bring in — kitchen
  • Refaced / repainted cabinets in warm greige; slim matte-black or unlacquered-brass pulls
  • One run of floating oak open shelving with stoneware
  • Handmade cream zellige backsplash; twin rattan pendants
Bring in — nook
  • Round oak pedestal table + cane bistro chairs (or a linen banquette)
  • Kilim runner, olive bud vase, a woven pendant to anchor the table

Tiny kitchen: stay light and reflective and limit open shelving to a single run so it never feels loaded.

Concept · Dining & fireplace
ROOM 0510'5" × 16'3" · 165 sq ft · wood fireplace

Gather around the fire

The largest room on the floor and the only one with a fireplace — make that the anchor. Limewash the surround, set a generous oak table beneath one big woven pendant, and mix the seating so it never feels formal.

Retire
  • Dark formal dining suite and matching china cabinet
  • Brass-and-crystal chandelier; dated tile/brick fireplace face
Bring in
  • Solid oak plank table with mixed seating — upholstered linen end chairs + cane sides
  • Oversized rattan / plaster pendant centred on the table
  • Limewashed fireplace + simple oak mantel: lean arched mirror, two ceramic vessels, taper candles
  • Low oak sideboard, striped wool flatweave, olive drapery, pampas

Big room, one material story: oak + plaster + rattan, repeated, keeps 165 sq ft feeling collected rather than sparse.

Concept · Powder room
ROOM 064'11" × 4'4" · 21 sq ft · 2-piece

The jewel-box moment

The smallest room is the safest place to be bold. With barely any square footage, a deep clay wall is a low-commitment, high-impact gesture — pure boho drama in a closet-sized space.

Retire
  • Builder vanity, chrome faucet and frameless flat mirror
  • Cool-white paint and bright overhead light
Bring in
  • Clay / terracotta limewash wall (or venetian plaster) for depth
  • Small floating oak vanity or console with a stoneware vessel sink
  • Unlacquered-brass faucet, arched rattan mirror, woven sconces on a warm bulb
  • Terracotta hand towels, handmade soap dish, one trailing plant

21 sq ft: one wallet-friendly wall finish changes the whole feel — spend here, save elsewhere.

From board to rooms

This is a direction, not a purchase order — a shared language for the finishes, palette and pieces so the whole main floor pulls together. Swap any single piece; the rules (three textures, light & leggy, one clay moment) keep it coherent.

  1. Confirm what stays — flooring, cabinet boxes, the fireplace — before buying.
  2. Lock the palette with real samples: oak, limewash, a clay paint, one brass.
  3. Start with the anchor pieces (family sofa, dining table) and layer textiles last.
